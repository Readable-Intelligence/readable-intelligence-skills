# Applying the existing review criteria

This coordinator instruction implements the user's reader-centered review requirement. It does not change deployment criteria, create a new acceptance gate, or waive an existing one. Apply it in every subsequent acceptance-review assignment and root reconciliation.

Professional terminology, accurate definitions and attribution, precise same-strength formal statements, and preserved hypotheses, types, signs, units, actions, equivalence levels and complete arguments remain hard constraints. A smoother sentence cannot compensate for a violation. Check consequential source-dependent claims in faithful primary-source views.

Judge the actual manuscript against the assigned part's conditions. Apply its permissions for unambiguous established notation, ordinary descriptions, compositional properties, imported notation and multiple equivalent formulations. Do not add a preferred sentence template, mandatory arrow display, exhaustive restatement of standard definitions, length target, word blacklist, or responsibility outside the assigned part.

For a proposed failure concerning exposition, identify the printed passage and explain the concrete ambiguity, missing information, misleading claim or unusable interface for the intended expert reader. Consider the surrounding prose, valid preceding setup and applicable field conventions. An existing geometric or prose definition can be sufficient without a second formal display. A private editor explanation cannot supply information missing from the manuscript. A general source citation is not automatically an import of every symbol in that source.

Keep optional improvements separate from required repairs. Repetition, rhythm, glossing of familiar notation, and choice among equivalent formulations can be discussed with their reader costs; preference alone is not a failure. Private inventories support an audit and need not produce matching sentences in the paper.

Independent reader reports supplement technical acceptance. Their discoveries must be saved before criteria are exposed; prior diagnoses, editor notes and verdicts remain withheld. They can expose either useful improvements or mechanical enforcement, but cannot waive hard constraints or stand in for a source/proof audit. The coordinator reconciles disagreements against actual manuscript evidence and preserves any withdrawn judgment and its correction.
