# Part 01 — Terminology and notation

Revise terminology and notation throughout the manuscript, including abstract, statements, proofs, captions, appendices and bibliography. Do not reorganize the paper or replace its mathematical claims. Clarifications of a term's scope and of an identification's mathematical type are within scope.

## Procedure

1. Read every definition and use. Build a working index of specialized names exactly as printed, including prefixes and stacked modifiers, conventions, symbols and structure-preserving identifications. Distinguish ordinary descriptions from technical names. For each convention-sensitive object, identify the extra structure actually consumed later. For a constructed subobject or subcategory, check that its stated defining closure supplies the actions or scalar structure required by subsequent operations; the ambient object having that structure does not supply it to every subobject.
2. Survey the primary literature across every technical component used by the manuscript. For a full research paper, inspect at least six relevant primary works, including the vocabulary-defining references, nearest substantive predecessors and representative usage beyond one source lineage. This is a breadth floor, not a sufficient condition or a license to add irrelevant citations. If fewer relevant works exist, document the bounded search and why. If a necessary primary source is inaccessible, try a faithful mathematical edition, an alternative host, or another applicable primary treatment; compare its actual definitions and hypotheses. A failed download alone does not exhaust this task, and an unresolved inspection never counts as verification. Inspect applicable definitions and surrounding hypotheses, not entire papers merely for page counts.
3. For every disputed, uncommon, adapted or convention-sensitive term, compare the manuscript's object with at least two relevant primary treatments when available. Give each exact candidate phrase its own decision entry: manuscript occurrences; the shortest relevant name copied literally from the opened source and its locator (or explicitly state that the source gives no name), kept separate from your construction paraphrase; matching definition and hypotheses; retain/replace/describe/unresolved; final wording. Do not bundle distinct phrases under one source verdict. Finding a construction under a different name verifies the construction, not the manuscript's name. Do not describe the source using the manuscript's disputed label and then count that description as the source's terminology. Determine whether the phrase names a mathematical kind, operation or cited result, or applies established properties with their established meanings. Use the compositional-restriction branch only when each modifier expresses an independently established property with its required scope available in the manuscript; verbatim attestation of the adjective combination is unnecessary. Familiar component words do not certify a fused prefix, compressed type name or nickname assigned to a cited result or operation. If the phrase introduces an additional convention or changes the source term, apply the naming decision in step 5; otherwise explain the restriction in ordinary prose when its scope is unclear. Check object/category level, ambient assumptions, topology or site when relevant, variance, operations, normalization and equivalence notion. A second comparison must address the same relevant object, property or convention; a work supplying an adjacent input is not that comparison. Before using a single-definition exception, inspect the defining work’s references and search for other primary treatments of the concept, including its established equivalent terminology. Different hypotheses or coefficient settings do not make a treatment irrelevant: compare the shared definition and record the differences without using it for a stronger claim. Before excluding a candidate treatment for a missing variant, search its full text, including appendices, for that variant and its equivalent terminology; inspect the relevant passages instead of treating its first formulation as exhaustive. Where only one relevant treatment is found after that bounded search, inspect its definition and independent mathematical use, explicitly recording the search and evidence limit. For a convention expressed by a formula, record the actual source formula from a faithful mathematical view and compare every factor, sign and degree with the manuscript, even when initially claiming a match. In the manuscript itself, attribute each imported part of a combined convention and distinguish any local choice; a correct separation only in source notes does not repair the printed attribution. Pin versions whenever numbering or conventions differ. Do not infer nonexistence from one failed phrase search or treat frequency as a definition.
4. Before deciding that a source has a different mathematical type or strength, confirm the decisive notation in the rendered original or reliable mathematical source for that exact version. Plain PDF extraction can detach arrow decorations, subscripts, signs and diagram labels. Search snippets and extracted text alone cannot establish such a discrepancy. Inspect the surrounding hypotheses too. If a web preview fails, try an available local PDF renderer or the exact-version mathematical source; a failed preview alone does not exhaust source access. If no faithful view is available, keep the consequential comparison unverified. Record the page or source locator and the reading modality for each consequential mismatch. An independent reviewer must make this check independently before accepting the mismatch.
5. Resolve each candidate by the following decision. If an established name already identifies the construction accurately, use that name with the correct convention; related mathematics under that name does not justify an altered label. Otherwise prefer an accurate plain clause stating the object, action or restriction. Retain a new local name only when existing terms demonstrably misdescribe a recurring object and the name materially improves repeated reasoning; define it as local and justify that distinction. This is the sole necessary-new-name exception and applies also to names formed by modifying existing terms. A local definition alone is insufficient. When choosing description, write the actual property, action or restriction in the manuscript. If a nonstandard phrase is used to quantify over a class, state its defining property or refer to the already defined family; an intended referent only in notes does not bind it. This requirement does not demand a new definition of an unambiguous standard property. Calling an unchanged compressed label “descriptive” in notes is not a replacement. A heading may describe a subject without naming a mathematical kind or assigning a name to a cited result or operation. A descriptive reference to a cited result states what it supplies alongside the citation; calling a retained nickname descriptive in notes does not do this. If necessary source evidence is unavailable, record the decision as unresolved rather than claiming either standardness or justified novelty.
6. Edit every affected occurrence. Define locally introduced or ambiguous symbols at their first nontrivial use, in preceding setup or an immediately attached defining clause; explain the roles of indices or parameters when their distinction matters. Established notation may rely on unambiguous field conventions. An explicit import need not repeat definitions; check that the preceding or immediately attached prose identifies the notation being adopted. A general convention citation or a later definition does not establish that import. Do not create an introductory glossary for familiar notation. When completing a declaration, recover its scalar field, ambient category and restrictions from the actual argument. Distinguish the domain where a map exists from the smaller domain where it has the claimed property. Supplying a broad type for a symbol must not turn a restricted equivalence, equality or estimate into a universal assertion; state the restriction at that assertion. Resolve assertions about “the datum”, “the structure” or similar referents to the object actually defined: a property of one component is not a property of the whole tuple. Disambiguate repeated notation only when it creates a real collision. Attribute separately an underlying-object identification and any additional multiplication, action or naturality structure.
7. Close the initial name index against the complete final manuscript, adding any newly introduced candidate names. Every candidate needs its own completed decision and final-occurrence check, including names retained unchanged; grouped component coverage cannot close an individual entry. After replacing or describing a phrase, search the final manuscript for its distinctive disputed name, including uses in other grammatical positions. Removing modifiers while retaining the same unsupported operation name does not complete the replacement. Give any retained name its own naming decision; ordinary compositional properties still follow step 3. Recompare the source definition with the final wording, then inspect its downstream uses for the structure actually consumed. Traverse the complete final manuscript in source order, considering every display and its surrounding prose, including unchanged passages. In changes.md record each display location and bind its nonstandard local symbols, including operation symbols, to the actual declaration or explicit notation import: full parameter signature, mathematical level, domain, normalization when relevant, first-use location and declaration location. At first use the declaration must precede or be immediately attached as in step 6; finding its definition later in the paper does not close that use. For a free local input, locate its printed binder or explicit import; reconstructing its type from the surrounding operations does not establish that it was bound. Declaring both operands does not declare an operation between them. When a familiar glyph is reused for a different kind of input or action, state that meaning in the manuscript if the existing convention does not cover it; an action’s existence or a private ledger interpretation is not a declaration of its printed alias. Unambiguous established operations need no redundant definition. A one-parameter object does not declare a tuple-indexed family, and an unnamed construction does not declare a new functor alias. A section-level “notation checked” row cannot replace this traversal. Guessing new data or recording “defined in this section” is not a declaration; repair the manuscript. Ordinary bound variables and unambiguous established notation need no artificial entries or glossary. Test newly supplied domains against the next asserted identity's hypotheses; well-typed sides do not prove a law. Save the per-name decisions, component coverage and unresolved issues in `sources.md`, and the actual repairs and final-occurrence checks in `changes.md`.

## Acceptance — all required

- Every technical component has inspected primary-source coverage. Every uncertain or convention-sensitive technical name has both located naming evidence and a matching definition comparison, has been replaced with the established name or an accurate descriptive clause, or satisfies the sole necessary-new-name exception in step 5 with a located distinction and an explicit local definition. A pointer to a related construction alone is not naming evidence; evidence limits are explicit.
- No unnecessary local technical name survives merely because it was defined or appeared in the original. No unverified replacement is advertised as standard.
- Every locally introduced or convention-sensitive symbol has an actual declaration or explicit imported scope; established unambiguous notation may use the field’s conventions. Every additional structure consumed later is specified. New declarations preserve the scalar field, category and hypotheses of the assertions using them; they do not broaden a restricted law merely because its formula is well-typed. Changing labels or indices has not silently changed the referent.
- Every claimed source discrepancy affecting type, equivalence, variance, signs or scope is confirmed against original mathematical notation and surrounding hypotheses by both editor and independent reviewer; uncertainty caused by extraction is not a mathematical gap.
- An identification carries only the structures actually established; a representation of an underlying object is not credited with an unspecified product or coherence.
- Every altered term has been checked against its source again after editing and against all actual downstream uses. All preservation conditions in the common contract hold.
