# restructure-paper: design and evidence

This skill was built and tested as `readable-duo` v12; the records below use that name (v12) for it, and `restructure-paper` for the previous, single-model skill.

**Naming (2026-10-22).** The old skill's "stages" are called *parts* of the standard: they are areas of the standard, not steps of the workflow. Acceptance conditions are numbered `P02.1` … `P06.4` (formerly `S…`). The introduction's account of how the main results are proved is called the *proof sketch*, and the reviewer's `reading.json` the *reading record*. Rows below that describe the old skill keep its word "stage".

## Two predecessors, one skill

**`restructure-paper`** is a single-model workflow of six editing stages. Each stage was accepted by an independent reviewer against written conditions, PASS or FAIL, in restricted reading views (statements without proofs, the introduction without the body, the main assembly without supporting proofs).
- **Strength:** its output explains. For example, its introduction gave the commutativity proof as three steps, each tied to the obstacle it removes, with plain glosses of new terms; its proof openings said why the method works.
- **Weaknesses:** it relied on one model family, ran no independent proof check of added steps, and was not automated.

**`readable-duo` v11** is a two-model, automated pipeline:
- literature retrieval and calibration of the paper's arrangement against comparable papers;
- GPT writing and checking (fidelity with script support; an independent proof check of every rewritten passage);
- Claude judging (an open-ended referee and a blind pairwise vote that decided acceptance), with strict isolation.

Its strength was global arrangement and checked mathematics: main results stated early, early deduction sections, verified added mechanisms. Its weakness surfaced on a real 23-page paper. Exposition accreted round by round:
- the introduction's commutativity sketch grew from 159 to 318 words of facts without reasons;
- proof openings became tables of contents;
- compressed noun phrases stood in for mechanisms;
- commentary on whether a cited work proves its results grew in five places.

v11 already gave its writer the restructure-paper stage texts, but only in the first pass. Every later round was steered by relative judgements, and **what decided acceptance was a vote of "which version is better", which rewards adding.**

v12 keeps v11's machinery as the engine and makes restructure-paper's standard the definition of done:
- the standard is kept verbatim in `standard/` (except part 05, replaced after tests, part 06's two references to it, and the renaming of the old skill's "stages" to parts; see below) and rendered into prompts from that one source;
- every round reviews the candidate against all 23 conditions of parts 02–06 (part 01's terminology work is replaced by the registrar's rule, below);
- the vote only guards against regressions.

## How a version is judged

**Acceptance review.** A fresh Claude session first writes `reading.json`, its reading record:
- what is claimed;
- each main proof's route from the introduction alone and from the statements alone;
- whether each construction's purpose was clear beforehand;
- every place it had to supply a step or guess a referent;
- every passage that gave it nothing new.

Only then may it open the standard. The tool log is checked for this order and the step is retried if it was violated. It then rules on every condition with located evidence; a FAIL states what must become true, not the sentence to write. This follows restructure-paper's independent reviewer, who saved discovery before seeing the criteria, so that the conditions sharpen judgement instead of replacing reading.

**Soundness.** The fidelity check (GPT, with script-flagged places it must rule on) and the independent proof check (GPT, strongest setting, judging only from the texts it is given) produce blocking findings. These accumulate in one ledger:
- **What counts against a version:** a finding counts against every version whose text still contains it unchanged.
- **How a finding clears:** only the check of the version that rewrote it can clear it.

**Regressions.** One blind vote compares the candidate with its predecessor (in a repair, with the current deliverable). It can only reject: when it finds the candidate worse with medium confidence or more overall, or with high confidence for one reader.

**Termination of a round.**
- **Repair loop:** each attempt must strictly lower the debt, a non-negative integer; after at most `max_repairs` attempts, one revert sheet restores the original's wording, which by R1's premise carries no debt.
- **The run as a whole:** it stops when all conditions pass with clean checks, when a cross-review round changes nothing, after two rejections, or at the round limit.

## Defences

| Failure | Detector | Correction | Evidence it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| An added proof step is false or unsupported | GPT proof check of every rewritten passage; Claude added if planted defects are missed | Repair loop; revert sheet | Planted defects in round 1 measure the checkers in every run |
| A rewritten passage escapes the check | Script: every changed proof paragraph is a region that needs a verdict | Retry until covered | Coverage enforced in every panel |
| An original formula or computation is deleted | Script: labelled displays of the original whose label and content are both gone; each needs a fidelity ruling | Lost → H → debt → repair loop restores it | Flags exactly the deleted formula in the v12 test, nothing in the version before it |
| A statement or qualification is lost or changes strength | Script flags plus GPT fidelity ruling on each | H finding → debt | Planted statement defects |
| A gap is closed by a new or stronger assumption | Fidelity check compares every assumption with the original's; the proof check rejects steps resting on one | H finding or `unsupported` → debt → repair loop | Added after the retest found one (`docs/TESTS.md`) |
| A condition of the standard is not judged | Script: a verdict for every condition id; FAIL needs a required repair | Retry | Stub tests |
| The reviewer reads the criteria before reading the paper | Claude tool log: `reading.json` written before any read of `criteria/` | Retry | Stub-incompatible; enforced on real runs |
| Exposition accretes | The cross review's reviewers (time spent reading counts as a cost), each checked by the other; the conditions; the vote's shorter-is-better rule | The next cross-review round | CR3 against M6 (row above) |
| Text copied from a reference; an uncheckable citation | Script detectors | Reported to the next cross-review round | — |
| A problem a check found is ignored by the revision | Script: each reviewer's list must decide every reported problem by id; the other reviewer judges each decision | Retry; the next panel checks again | Stub tests |
| The cross review reads v12's records, or finds the paper itself online | Vault locked while it runs; its audit flags reads of the run directory and the package, and searches or downloads that name the paper | Retry, then stop | Lock probe and audit unit test (`docs/TESTS.md`) |
| One family's taste decides | Final acceptance and final comparison by both families | Fallback to the original only if both prefer it | — |
| Isolation breaks | Sync-folder refusal, lock test, lock watcher, tool-call audit; GPT steps in their own Codex home | Refused or stopped | The watcher caught iCloud undoing the lock (v11); the audit caught a step reading a user-installed Codex skill (v12 test), which the separate Codex home now prevents |

## Residual risks

- **Codex's bundled system skills.** GPT steps run with their own Codex home (`run_dir/_codex_home`, holding only a link to the login), so the user's global `AGENTS.md`, installed skills, memories and session history do not reach them. Before this, `--ignore-user-config` alone let `~/.codex/AGENTS.md` steer 20 steps, and a step read a user-installed skill (the audit caught it). Codex's four bundled system skills (image generation, OpenAI docs, skill creation and installation) remain visible; no step has used them.
- **Reviewer noise.** Each round's acceptance review is a single reviewer instance. The final acceptance by two families shows the spread. The cross review's coverage also varies between runs; more rounds find more.
- **The cross review and v12's rules.** The cross review follows its own standard (`cross_review/prompts/standard.md`), not `hard_rules.md`. v12's constraints reach it only through the paragraph at the top of the reported problems (statements keep their strength; a step of the original is removed only with a reason) and through the checks of its result. A rule it does not know, such as R7 on discussing cited works, is enforced only where a check catches its breach.
- **A rejected round passes nothing on.** After a rejection, the next round reviews the same base with fresh reviewers; it is not told what the rejected revision did wrong.
- **The cross review's command lines** always use Claude's `--restricted --safe-mode --permission-prompts none`; v12's preflight, which can drop an option the installed CLI rejects, does not adjust them.
- **Conditions that ask about editing records.** Some conditions (P02.5, P03.3) ask whether the editor checked something. A reviewer without those records may mark them UNVERIFIED. UNVERIFIED does not block, and it is reported.
- **GPT's final acceptance review.** Its reading-first order is instructed, not enforced (Codex's event log does not map reliably to file reads).
- **Running conditions.** Root, launching from inside Claude Code (the `~/.claude` locks are skipped), and search snippets during retrieval, as in v11.
