# Part 02 — Formal statements

Rewrite every theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary and immediately necessary setup. Read their proofs and all uses to determine the exact claim. Preserve the mathematics; do not preserve the old sentence form or introduced symbols by default. Make corresponding downstream wording/reference repairs, without reorganizing entire proof branches in this part.

## Procedure

1. Recover each result's logical contract from the whole original: its subject, fixed auxiliary data, assumptions, usable conclusion and consumers, including definitions and standing conventions explicitly incorporated by reference. Locate the original passages binding each object, additional structure and identification. Unpack each assumed structured object into the components and maps actually needed by the result: its underlying objects, any base or projection, actions and specified identifications. For each indispensable relationship, record the exact revised statement or preceding clause expressing that relationship. An identification of the outer object, even with some extra structure preserved, does not by itself identify its chosen base, projection or other nested component. Test those bindings separately against the original contract. Giving a datum’s provenance, existence or coefficient field does not supply them. Recover the full ambient scope from that setup before enumerating an implicit parameter or choosing a setting: cases not used by a later consumer still belong to the original result. Check both original-to-revision and revision-to-original scope, not only applicability at the consumers. Do this before asking whether the revised statement is readable in isolation from its proof. An existing contextual binding may need to be made explicit in preceding setup; this clarification is not a new assumption. Do not replace the original contract by a weaker isolated-sentence reading and then report the resulting missing binding as a mathematical gap. Conversely, a proof's need alone supplies no hypothesis: an underlying-object isomorphism does not identify additional structures unless the original actually incorporates that identification. If the original supports conflicting contracts, record the conflict without guessing an additional theorem. Preserve a property of the specified structured object as such, rather than existence of some other structure. Put indispensable assumed bindings in the statement or reachable preceding setup; a forward reference may explain them but must not be their sole readable location in the revision. The derivation of a model or identification belongs in the proof; its presence or naming in the statement is decided by the output role and clarity comparison in steps 2–3. Check each invocation against this recovered contract; do not silently strengthen or weaken it.
2. Inventory the named objects and quantities in every result and its preceding setup, including unchanged abbreviations and normalization definitions. Classify each by its actual role: input parameter needed to express hypotheses; substantive output or compatibility data; existential witness used to express the conclusion; or proof construction. Naming an input family can be useful when its components, maps or simultaneous conditions must be specified precisely. Such a name need not reappear in the conclusion or a later result. Compare an existential-witness formulation with the direct assertion at the same strength. Keep a conventional witness or equivalence display when it communicates the conclusion as clearly; remove the name when it adds avoidable declaration or memory burden. Mere availability of a paraphrase is not a reason to rewrite. Move names for proof-only constructions into the proof. Distinguish bare existence of a model from an explicit computational realization: a concrete complex, geometric model, formula or comparison whose specified data lets readers compute or apply the result can be substantive output. Its symbols may remain when they make that content clearer, including when its consumer is another proof. Replacing them by a long verbal encoding is not automatically an improvement. For a precursor related to the actual subject by a fixed conversion, use the complete-formulation comparison in step 3; being defined or reused does not by itself make that precursor substantive output. Repeating a disposable name later does not rescue it. Apply the same test to preceding setup: hide the proof, remove each construction block in turn, and ask which definitions or hypotheses the result and its actual consumers still need. If a named precursor contributes only to establishing this result, rather than specifying its assumptions or substantive output, move its definition and formula into the proof. Its use in this result’s own proof alone is not a reason to retain it in preceding setup. A short plain-language explanation of the mathematical subject may remain, but it must not reintroduce the removable construction or its notation as “motivation”.
3. Draft both a direct subject-centered formulation and, when relevant, a witness formulation. Compare them at exactly the same mathematical strength. The direct formulation may say “admits a ... model” or “is equivalent, as ..., to ...”; it need not claim the supplied presentation already has a strict structure. Test each candidate name separately: a useful computational model does not justify every precursor name surrounding it. If a statement computes an auxiliary quantity related to its actual subject by a fixed conversion, write the complete direct formula with that conversion and compare the declaration burden. Keep the auxiliary target name only if it adds separate usable data or makes the full computation clearer; the model's usefulness alone is not its justification. Choose the clearest economical formulation preserving the conclusion and precise hypotheses; minimizing the number of symbols is not the objective. Test whether the rewrite removes a definition the reader would otherwise have to remember, rather than merely replacing useful indexed notation with longer prose. For every output-witness or precursor candidate in that inventory, including unchanged abbreviations, record the complete direct alternative and individual retention reason. Familiar notation and a decision about a neighboring useful object do not close the candidate's own test.
4. Apply economy to assertions as well as names. Move an assertion serving only as a proof mechanism into the proof, preserving its full mathematical content there; removing its notation or renaming it as an intrinsic action does not remove the assertion. Keep a mechanism as formal output when its specified data or separate use makes it substantive under steps 2–3. Remove restatements of the named categorical equivalence and unrelated consequences from formal conclusions. Keep a useful explanatory consequence nearby in prose when it materially clarifies the conclusion. Do not delete a hypothesis, important dependence, noncanonicity or unit/action qualification just because it is short enough to overlook. When extracting or checking a definition, verify its actual mathematical type from its construction, not merely that a domain and codomain have been written. Carry its well-definedness restrictions with it. Separately check the domain of every identity or equivalence asserted about it: an operation can exist on a larger class than the class where that law holds. Compare the quantified objects with the hypotheses of the cited or preceding argument, including boundary and sign cases; a displayed bound need not imply the original domain restriction or “sufficiently large” qualification.
5. Check every result as a reader would encounter it: from its statement and preceding explicit setup, without reading its proof. Then check each actual invocation against that statement. Traverse each complete proof from beginning to end, including unchanged formulas, and trace the first uses of its statement notation in the rest of the manuscript, including introductory formulas. For every display in this traversal, identify the local objects, maps and actions not bound inside it, and resolve each to an actual declaration or explicit notation import. At a first nontrivial use, the necessary meaning must be in preceding setup or an immediately attached defining clause; an unreferenced definition that appears later does not supply a declaration there. Do not restrict this scan to theorem inputs/outputs or symbols affected by editing. Record the display label or source location, each nonstandard local symbol's full parameter signature, domain and normalization, and its declaration location. A one-parameter object does not declare a family with extra parameters; a formula under another name does not declare an alias; a definition on one space does not by itself define the same symbol on a different quotient. Unambiguous established notation may use field conventions and ordinary bound variables need no artificial entries. A result-level “checked” row cannot replace this display-level traversal. Repair missing or incorrectly typed bindings in the manuscript. A declaration entry stays unresolved until its construction and each claimed law have been checked on the stated domain. When a proof invokes an assumed compatibility or component identification, link that use to the exact preceding clause recorded in step 1; the enclosing structure’s name or a generic citation alone does not locate that binding. Only print the components actually needed to check this result; do not repeat an entire standard definition or move identifications established by the proof into its assumptions. Use this same traversal to close the name decisions against the final source. Link every named definition in a statement or its necessary setup to its role decision in steps 2–3, including names newly introduced during declaration repair. For a fixed conversion, check each intermediate named quantity separately. A successful declaration entry does not establish a reason to retain that name; an omitted precursor decision remains open. Include the concise declaration inventory, these links and display coverage in changes.md; do not create another inventory or terminology survey. Mathematical meaning must agree in both directions between original and replacement.

## Acceptance — all required

- Each result states a usable conclusion about its subject with the exact assumptions and structure/equivalence level.
- Every output witness and proof-device name passes the same-strength comparison. A conventional existential witness may remain when it expresses the result as clearly as the direct alternative. Mere repetition, use solely in constructing the proof, or comparison with an incorrectly strengthened direct alternative does not justify retaining a name that adds avoidable burden. Explicit computational output may use notation when a verbal replacement would obscure the same data. Useful input notation that makes several hypotheses or compatibility conditions precise is permitted; its absence from the conclusion or later statements is not a failure.
- No hypothesis or output needed downstream is hidden only in the proof. The result and its reachable preceding setup suffice to check its uses.
- Redundant commentary, proof construction instructions and unnecessary conclusion bundles have been removed; necessary distinctions remain expressible in normal mathematical prose.
- Every changed result is mapped to its proof and consumers, with the same bound objects, specified structures, identifications and full original ambient scope, including cases not used later; no fixed datum has become an existentially available alternative. Locally introduced notation throughout the statements, their complete proofs and actual uses is declared, whether inherited or moved; no internal proof display is exempt. There is no strengthened claim, silently weakened qualification or lost complete argument.
